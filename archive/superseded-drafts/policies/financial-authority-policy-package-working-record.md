## Finance Committee Decisions Recorded

- Pastor approval up to `$250`.
- Pastor and Finance Committee Chair approval from `$251-$1,000`.
- Finance Committee approval from `$1,001-$3,000`.
- Church approval above `$3,000`.
- Reasonable pricing under `$1,000`, two quotes from `$1,000-$3,000` when practical, and three written quotes above `$3,000` unless only one qualified vendor exists.
- A special rule for purchases above `$25,000`: two-thirds vote at a special-called meeting with a 30% quorum based on current-year average Sunday morning attendance.
- Contract signatures only when authorized by the church or Finance Committee.
- Emergency authority for Deacons, Building and Grounds Committee, and Finance Committee to protect people, prevent major property damage, or restore essential services.
- Reimbursement requests due within 15 days with receipt, ministry purpose, budget category, and required approvals.
- Card issuance to people deemed necessary, limits set by the church or Finance Committee, monthly statement review, no personal use, no sharing, and automatic cancellation for sharing.
- Financial-interest disclosure and abstention.
- Use of the church's Missouri sales-tax exemption when practical.

## Changes From The Earlier Drafts

- The reimbursement period changed from 30 days to 15 days.
- Budgeted items and designated funds were both named as still subject to approval levels.
- Emergency authority was assigned to Deacons, Building and Grounds Committee, and Finance Committee.
- The card-sharing response changed to automatic card cancellation and immediate loss of custody.
- A `$25,000` special-called-meeting rule, two-thirds vote, and 30% attendance-based quorum were added.
- Several detailed controls from the earlier drafts were not included in the approved recommendation.

## Required Bylaw Reconciliation

The 2018 governing documents state:

- Constitution Article 5: church business will be voted on in the regularly scheduled business meeting.
- Bylaws Article 10: new church policies require at least two weeks' notice and a three-fourths vote of members present at the business meeting.
- The governing documents do not state the 30% attendance-based quorum included in the Finance Committee recommendation.

Before the recommendation is presented as effective policy, leadership should determine whether the special-called-meeting, two-thirds-vote, and 30%-quorum provisions require revision, a bylaw amendment, or separate congregational action.

## Tax Verification

The approved recommendation ties Missouri sales-tax exemption directly to federal 501(c)(3) status. The [Missouri Department of Revenue](https://dor.mo.gov/faq/taxation/business/sales-use-tax-exemptions.html) states that federal 501(c) status does not guarantee Missouri sales-tax exemption. KBC should confirm that it has a current Missouri Sales and Use Tax Exemption Letter and assign custody of that documentation.

## Follow-Up Controls Not Included In The Approved Text

These items should not be silently added to the approved policy. They are practical gaps for Finance Committee, leadership, or the congregation to consider through an implementation decision or future revision:

- Who independently approves a purchase when the Pastor is the requester or beneficiary.
- Whether approval thresholds apply to the total annual or contractual commitment rather than one invoice or monthly payment.
- Which specific people may sign contracts and how setup costs, renewals, cancellation terms, and termination penalties are reviewed.
- How quote exceptions other than a single qualified vendor are documented.
- Whether an individual Deacon or committee member may authorize an emergency purchase, what dollar limit applies, and what documentation and after-the-fact review are required.
- What happens when a reimbursement is late, a receipt is missing, or required approval was not obtained before purchase.
- Who may receive a card, what limits apply, how lost or compromised cards are reported, and how disputed or unclear transactions are handled.
- How approval, payment, recording, reconciliation, and monthly review duties remain separated.
- How conflict-of-interest disclosures and abstentions are documented.
